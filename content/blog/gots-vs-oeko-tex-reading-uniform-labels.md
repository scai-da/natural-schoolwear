You've decided you want safer, gentler school uniforms. You start reading labels and swing tags, and two names keep appearing: GOTS and OEKO-TEX. Both sound trustworthy. Both suggest the product is safer. But they're not the same, and knowing the difference helps you shop with confidence.

### The short version

- **OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100** tests the finished product for harmful substances.
- **GOTS** certifies that the product is organic and controls what can be used at every stage of making it, including social and environmental criteria.

One checks what's in the final garment. The other controls the whole process.

### OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100

OEKO-TEX is an international association that runs several labels. The one you'll see most is STANDARD 100.

**What it means:** every component of the product (fabric, thread, buttons, labels) has been tested by an independent lab against a long list of regulated and unregulated harmful substances, such as certain dyes, formaldehyde, heavy metals and pesticide residues.

**Product classes:** limits are strictest for products for babies and toddlers (Class I), then items worn against skin (Class II), which covers most school uniforms.

**What it doesn't mean:** OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 says nothing about whether the cotton was grown organically, how the factory treats its workers, or its environmental impact. A 100% conventional polyester shirt can carry the label if it passes the tests.

### GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard)

**What it means:** the product contains certified organic fibre and was processed under strict rules from the raw fibre through to the finished garment.

**Chemical rules:** GOTS bans a list of hazardous substances outright, including formaldehyde, PFAS, heavy metals, certain azo dyes and chlorine bleach. All dyes and auxiliaries must be pre-approved against toxicity criteria.

**Beyond chemicals:** GOTS includes wastewater treatment requirements and social criteria such as safe working conditions and no child labour.

**Two label grades:** "organic" means at least 95% certified organic fibre. "Made with X% organic" means at least 70%.

### Side by side

|  | GOTS | OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organic fibre required | Yes, at least 70% | No |
| Tests finished product for harmful substances | Yes, with residue limits | Yes, the core of the label |
| Bans hazardous inputs during processing | Yes | Not as process rules |
| Bans PFAS and formaldehyde | Yes, both | Limits formaldehyde; PFAS restricted |
| Social and environmental rules | Yes | No |
| Covers the whole supply chain | Yes | Finished product |

### How to check a label is real

Genuine certifications come with a licence or certificate number.

- **GOTS:** look for the licence number and certifier name near the logo, then search the GOTS public database.
- **OEKO-TEX:** look for the label number and test institute, then enter it in the label check tool on the OEKO-TEX website.

If there's a logo but no number, ask the brand for it.

### Which should you look for?

Both are good signs. OEKO-TEX is a useful baseline for any fabric, including synthetics. GOTS goes further for parents who want organic fibre, tighter chemical rules across production, and ethical manufacturing.
