You've read about PFAS, formaldehyde resins, disperse dyes and breathable fabrics. You'd like your child's school to offer better options. But school uniforms are set by policy, supplied under contracts, and steeped in tradition. Where do you start?

The encouraging news: schools change uniforms more often than you'd think, and parents are often the reason.

### Understand how your school decides

Uniform decisions usually involve a mix of:

- **The principal**, who signs off on uniform policy
- **The school council or board**
- **The P&C or P&F**, which often runs the uniform shop or second-hand shop
- **A uniform committee**, if your school has one
- **The uniform supplier**, under a contract that may run for several years

Find out who's responsible and when the supplier contract is next up for review. That's often the best window for change.

### Start with a small, specific ask

A request to overhaul the whole uniform can feel overwhelming. A small, practical ask is easier to say yes to. For example:

- Adding an **organic cotton alternative** for the summer shirt or polo
- Allowing a **cotton sport top** as an option alongside polyester
- Asking the supplier to confirm whether **stain-resistant or PFAS finishes** are used
- Including **OEKO-TEX or GOTS certification** as a preference in the next supplier tender
- Permitting a **cotton layer under wool jumpers** for children with sensitive skin

Optional additions mean no family is forced to replace what they already own.

### Bring evidence, not alarm

Schools respond well to calm, well-sourced information. Useful points to share:

- The 2022 peer-reviewed study that found PFAS in every stain-resistant school uniform tested
- That Australia banned three PFAS in 2025, but many others remain permitted
- Australian research showing eczema affects a large share of young children
- That cotton generally offers better air and moisture flow than standard polyester

Keep it to one page, link the sources, and avoid exaggerating. A measured tone builds credibility.

### Address the common concerns

Expect questions, and have answers ready:

- **Cost:** Organic uniforms can cost more upfront, but durable garments last longer and can be resold through the second-hand shop. Optional items let families choose.
- **Durability:** Well-made, tightly woven cotton is tough. Ask suppliers about fabric weight and wash testing.
- **Stains and ironing:** Without stain or wrinkle finishes, uniforms need ordinary washing and a shake before drying. Most families manage easily.
- **Consistency:** Alternatives can be made in the same colours and with the same logo so the uniform still looks unified.

### Build support

One parent asking is a request. Ten parents asking is a pattern. Talk to other families, especially those with children who have eczema or sensitive skin. A short survey through the P&C can show how many families are interested. Students' voices matter too: in some schools, student feedback on comfort has driven hot-weather uniform changes.

### Be patient

Uniform changes take time, especially with supplier contracts. Even if the answer is "not this year", getting the issue onto the agenda for the next review is a real win.
