One of the most common questions parents ask about organic cotton uniforms is: will they hold up? It's a fair question. School uniforms go through a lot. The good news is that well-made cotton is durable, and with a few simple habits, natural uniforms can last the year and be handed down after.

### Before first wear

**Wash it first.** This is good practice for any new clothing, organic or not. A first wash removes loose dye and any residues from manufacturing, packing and shipping, and softens the fabric. For children with sensitive skin, it's especially worthwhile.

### Everyday washing

- **Wash cold or warm, not hot.** 30 to 40°C is plenty for everyday school dirt and helps prevent shrinkage and fading.
- **Turn garments inside out.** It protects colour and the outer surface from rubbing.
- **Use a gentle, fragrance-free detergent.** Natural fibres don't need harsh products, and fragrance is a common skin irritant.
- **Skip fabric softener.** Cotton softens naturally with washing. Softeners can leave residue on fabric and skin, and reduce absorbency.
- **Don't overload the machine.** Clothes need room to move to get clean.

### Stain care without stain-resistant coatings

Natural Schoolwear is being designed without stain-repellent finishes, so it helps to have a simple routine:

1. **Act fast.** Rinse fresh stains in cold water as soon as you can. Hot water can set protein stains like milk, egg and blood.
2. **Rub with laundry soap.** A bar of plain laundry soap on a damp stain lifts most food, grass and dirt marks.
3. **Soak tough stains.** A soak in cold water with a scoop of oxygen-based cleaner works on many stains. Check it's suitable for coloured fabric.
4. **Let the sun help.** Drying whites in the sun gently lifts many remaining marks.

For texta and paint, check the product's label: most school-grade markers and paints are washable with soap and water.

### Drying

- **Line-dry where possible.** It's gentler on fibres, saves energy and makes the most of the Australian sun.
- **Dry colours in the shade or inside out** to reduce fading.
- **If tumble-drying, use low heat** and remove while slightly damp to reduce shrinkage and creasing.

### Keeping it neat without wrinkle-free finishes

Without chemical resins, cotton can crease. Some easy tricks:

- Shake garments firmly before hanging
- Hang shirts and dresses on hangers to dry, so they dry in shape
- Smooth collars and plackets by hand while damp
- Fold or hang promptly once dry
- If needed, a quick press with a warm iron takes a minute

Most families find no ironing needed for everyday school wear.

### Repairs and hand-me-downs

Cotton is easy to mend. Sew on loose buttons quickly, patch worn knees, and reinforce seams before they split. When your child outgrows a piece, pass it on to a younger sibling, a friend, or your school's second-hand shop.

### Why care matters

The longer a uniform lasts, the better it is for your budget and the planet. A durable organic uniform that serves two or three children is far better value, and far less waste, than several cheaper items replaced each year.
