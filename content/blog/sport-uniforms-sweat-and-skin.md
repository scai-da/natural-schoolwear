Sport uniform days are many kids' favourite days. Shorts, a polo and runners instead of a shirt and school shoes. But they're also the days when skin works hardest: more running, more sweat, more friction. And in many Australian schools, children wear their sport uniform all day, not just for a single PE lesson.

### Why sport uniforms are mostly polyester

School sport uniforms are almost always polyester or polyester blends. There are solid reasons: polyester is durable, holds bright house colours, resists stretching, and dries fast. Some performance polyester is engineered to wick sweat away from skin during hard exercise, and it can do that very well.

The question is whether the polyester in your child's sport polo is that kind of engineered performance fabric, or simply a budget polyester knit. Often it's the latter.

### The long-day problem

Performance fabrics are designed for an hour or two of intense activity, followed by a shower and a change. A school sport day looks different:

- A burst of sport in the morning
- Several hours of sitting in class while sweat dries into the fabric
- More play at lunch
- Afternoon lessons, then the trip home

That's a lot of hours in a garment that has been sweated in, dried, and sweated in again. For most kids it's just a bit uncomfortable. For children with eczema or sensitive skin, it can mean itch and irritation where the fabric sits against damp skin.

Some schools have even noticed this themselves: one UK school reviewing its hot weather rules concluded its black polyester PE tops were hotter to wear than its white cotton school shirts.

### Dyes and colours

Bright house colours and dark sport uniforms in polyester are usually coloured with disperse dyes. Some of these are recognised as among the more common causes of clothing-related skin allergy. If your child develops a rash that follows the outline of their sport top or shorts, it's worth mentioning to your GP.

### Keeping active kids comfortable

1. **Add a cotton layer where allowed.** A thin cotton singlet or crop under a polyester polo can soak up sweat and protect sensitive skin.
2. **Choose cotton or cotton-rich options** if your school's supplier offers them.
3. **Pack a spare top** for after PE on hot days, if your school allows a change.
4. **Watch the friction zones.** Inner thighs, underarms and waistbands are prone to chafing. Well-fitting, soft shorts help.
5. **Wash promptly.** Don't let sweaty sport uniforms sit in the bag overnight. Bacteria and odour build quickly.
6. **Rinse well.** Detergent residue plus sweat can irritate skin.

### What about cotton for sport?

Cotton isn't perfect for intense exercise. It absorbs sweat well but dries slowly, so a fully soaked cotton shirt can feel heavy. But for the kind of activity most primary school children do, a breathable cotton knit is comfortable, gentle and practical, especially across a long day.

The ideal is a balance: soft natural fibres that handle everyday sweat, cut for movement, without chemical finishes.
