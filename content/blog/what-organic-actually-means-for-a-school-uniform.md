"Organic" is one of those words that sounds reassuring but can be slippery. On food, it usually means certified farming practices. On clothing, it can mean anything from a fully certified garment to a shirt with a small percentage of organic fibre and a lot of marketing.

Here's how to tell the difference.

### Organic starts in the field

Organic cotton is grown without synthetic pesticides, synthetic fertilisers or genetically modified seed. Farmers use methods that build soil health and support biodiversity instead. That's good news for the farmers, the farmworkers and the land.

But here's the part many people miss: growing cotton organically says nothing about what happens next.

### The journey from fibre to uniform

Between the cotton field and your child's wardrobe, fibre goes through spinning, weaving or knitting, bleaching, dyeing, finishing and sewing. Each stage can involve chemicals: bleaches, dyes, softeners, resins, repellents and more.

A shirt can be made from organic cotton and still be:

- Bleached with chlorine
- Dyed with allergenic or azo dyes
- Treated with formaldehyde resins to resist wrinkles
- Coated with a stain-repellent finish

So "made with organic cotton" alone doesn't tell you whether the finished garment is gentle on skin.

### Why certification matters

This is where the **Global Organic Textile Standard (GOTS)** comes in. GOTS is the leading international standard for organic textiles, and it covers the whole supply chain, from processing the fibre to the finished product. To carry the GOTS label, a product must:

- Contain at least 70% certified organic fibre (95% for the "organic" grade label)
- Use only approved chemical inputs that meet strict toxicity criteria
- Avoid banned substances, including formaldehyde, PFAS, heavy metals, azo dyes that release harmful amines, and chlorine bleaching
- Meet wastewater treatment requirements at wet-processing facilities
- Meet social criteria such as fair working conditions and no child labour

Every stage of the supply chain is inspected by an independent certifier. You can check a GOTS licence number on the GOTS public database.

### Red flags on labels

Be cautious when you see:

- **"Organic" with no certification logo or licence number**
- **"Made with organic cotton"** without saying how much
- **"Natural" or "eco"** with no supporting detail
- **Organic cotton blended with polyester and a stain-resistant finish**

These claims aren't necessarily untrue, but they don't give you enough to judge.

### Why it matters for school uniforms

A certified organic uniform gives parents something rare: confidence about what's in the garment without needing a chemistry degree. For a piece of clothing worn for thousands of hours, that's worth a lot.

It also matters in Australia because the ACCC takes misleading environmental and health claims seriously. Certification is how genuine brands back up what they say.
