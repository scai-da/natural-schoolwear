If you've read about PFAS in school uniforms, you might be wondering: doesn't Australia ban these chemicals? The answer is partly yes, and it's worth understanding where the line sits.

### What changed on 1 July 2025

From 1 July 2025, three PFAS chemicals were listed on Schedule 7 of the **Industrial Chemicals Environmental Management Standard (IChEMS)**. Schedule 7 is the strictest category, covering chemicals that cannot be imported, manufactured, used or exported because of their risk to the environment. The three are:

- **PFOS** (perfluorooctane sulfonic acid)
- **PFOA** (perfluorooctanoic acid)
- **PFHxS** (perfluorohexane sulfonic acid)

These are older, well-studied PFAS that have been used in firefighting foams, non-stick coatings, packaging and, historically, water- and stain-repellent textiles. The ban covers products and packaging containing them, including textiles.

It's a genuine step forward, and brings Australia closer to international commitments under the Stockholm Convention on persistent pollutants.

### What the rules don't cover

The PFAS family includes thousands of chemicals. The 2025 ban targets three. Many others remain permitted.

This matters for uniforms. In the 2022 North American school uniform study, the most abundant PFAS found was **6:2 fluorotelomer alcohol (6:2 FTOH)**. That compound belongs to a newer generation of "short-chain" PFAS that industry moved to as PFOA and PFOS were phased out. It's not one of the three banned in Australia.

In other words, a stain-resistant uniform can comply with the Australian ban and still contain PFAS.

### Not every state has adopted it yet

IChEMS is a national standard, but each state and territory has to adopt it into its own law. A Senate inquiry reported that, as of April 2025, Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria had adopted IChEMS, the ACT, Tasmania and South Australia had partially adopted it, and Western Australia and the Northern Territory had not yet adopted it.

The same inquiry noted that IChEMS doesn't spell out how businesses should verify that imported products are free of banned PFAS. Most school uniforms sold in Australia are made overseas, so that gap matters.

### Where it's heading

The Australian Senate's Select Committee on PFAS released its final report in November 2025, with recommendations that could significantly change how PFAS are regulated. The Department of Environment has said all PFAS are expected to be listed on the IChEMS Register eventually.

Overseas, some jurisdictions have gone further. New York and California have passed laws restricting intentionally added PFAS in clothing.

### What parents can do now

Regulation will keep evolving. Until it catches up, you can make choices that avoid PFAS regardless of which specific compounds are banned:

- **Avoid stain-resistant, stain-release and water-repellent uniform items**
- **Ask suppliers directly** whether any fluorinated or PFAS-based finish was used
- **Choose GOTS-certified textiles**, which ban PFAS as a class rather than one compound at a time
- **Raise it with your school** when uniform contracts are reviewed
